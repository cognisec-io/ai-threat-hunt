Quick Start

The core idea in under a minute, before you get into the weeds.

What & Why

What it is: Simulating adversarial behavior against a GenAI system — like an LLM or an agent — to uncover vulnerabilities in security, safety, and trust before an attacker does. Thinking like an attacker to find the flaws first.

Why it's different: Traditional security testing asks "can I break into the server?" GenAI red teaming also asks "can I get the model to produce something harmful, false, or deceptive?" — the model's own output is part of the attack surface, not just the infrastructure around it.

Initial Steps
01Define objectives & scope. Start with your most business-critical or sensitive-data-handling applications. Get started and show value before trying to cover everything.
02Assemble the team. AI engineers, cybersecurity experts, and — where possible — ethics or compliance specialists. Diversity of skill sets is what catches diverse issues.
03Threat model. How would an attacker actually exploit this specific application? Align scenarios to your highest-priority risks — an internal tool and a public-facing chatbot don't carry the same risk.
04Address the full stack. Model weaknesses, implementation guardrails, system-wide integration points, and real-time human/agent interaction — all four, not just the model.
05Use tools & frameworks. Start with basic prompt-testing and adversarial-query tooling rather than building everything from scratch.
06Document and report. Every vulnerability, exploit scenario, and weakness — with clear, actionable remediation steps, not just a list of problems.
07Debrief and improve continuously. Red teaming isn't a one-time event — re-test after fixes and fold periodic checks into the AI lifecycle as models and environments evolve.
